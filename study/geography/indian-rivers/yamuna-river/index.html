1. Origin and Formation

  • Origin: Yamunotri Glacier, Uttarakhand.
  • Location: Southwestern slopes of the Banderpooch peaks in the Lower Himalayas.
  • Elevation: About 6,387 metres.
  • Length: About 1,376 km.
  • Type: Himalayan, perennial river.
  • Final confluence: Ganga at Prayagraj.
Most Important: Yamuna originates from the Yamunotri Glacier and joins the Ganga at Prayagraj.

2. Course of the Yamuna

  • The Yamuna flows through the Himalayan region of Uttarakhand.
  • It receives important Himalayan tributaries such as Tons and Giri.
  • It enters the plains near the Yamuna–Shivalik foothill region.
  • It forms parts of the boundary between Haryana and Uttar Pradesh.
  • It passes through or near important cities including Delhi, Mathura, Agra and Etawah.
  • Below Etawah, it receives major Peninsular tributaries such as Chambal, Sindh, Betwa and Ken.
  • Finally, it joins the Ganga at Prayagraj.

3. Major Tributaries of the Yamuna

Right-bank Tributaries

Tributary Important Exam Fact
Tons Largest Himalayan tributary of the Yamuna; joins the Yamuna below Kalsi.
Giri Himalayan tributary; joins the Yamuna near Paonta Sahib.
Chambal Major Peninsular tributary and the most important tributary of the Yamuna; joins near the Etawah region.
Sindh Important Peninsular tributary of the Yamuna.
Betwa Major Peninsular tributary; joins the Yamuna near Hamirpur.
Ken Important Peninsular tributary; joins the Yamuna near Chilla in Uttar Pradesh.

Left-bank Tributaries

Tributary Important Exam Fact
Hindon Important western tributary; joins the Yamuna near the Delhi–Uttar Pradesh region.
Hanuman Ganga Minor tributary associated with the upper Yamuna system.
Rind Important minor left-bank tributary in Uttar Pradesh.
Sengar Minor left-bank tributary of the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh.
Bank-wise Memory:

Right Bank: Tons → Giri → Chambal → Sindh → Betwa → Ken

Left Bank: Hindon → Hanuman Ganga → Rind → Sengar

Right and left bank are determined while facing downstream in the direction of river flow.

Important West-to-East Sequence

Chambal → Sindh → Betwa → Ken

Memory Trick:
C – S – B – K
“Chal Sindh Betwa Ken”

This west-to-east sequence is particularly useful for UPPSC/UPPCS preliminary examinations.

4. Yamuna River Basin

Fact Data
River length 1,376 km
Indian drainage basin About 3.66 lakh sq. km
Major states / regions Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh and parts of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh
Final drainage Ganga at Prayagraj
Geography Fact: Yamuna is the longest river in India that does not directly flow into the sea; instead, it joins the Ganga at Prayagraj.

7. Yamuna–Ganga Confluence

The Yamuna finally joins the Ganga at Prayagraj, Uttar Pradesh. The confluence is popularly known as the Triveni Sangam.

Most Important for UPPCS:

Yamuna → Ganga at Prayagraj

Not at Varanasi.

After the confluence, the combined flow continues eastward as part of the Ganga system and eventually reaches the Bay of Bengal.

8. Common Exam Traps

  • Yamuna is a tributary of Ganga, not an independent river draining directly into the sea.
  • Yamuna joins Ganga at Prayagraj, not Varanasi.
  • Chambal is a tributary of Yamuna, not directly of Ganga.
  • Betwa, Ken and Sindh are tributaries of Yamuna.
  • Tons is a Yamuna tributary, not a direct Ganga tributary.
  • Right/left bank must be determined while facing downstream.
  • The Yamuna Basin is much larger than the area occupied by the Yamuna's main channel.

9. Quick Revision Facts

  • Origin: Yamunotri Glacier
  • Mountain region: Banderpooch massif
  • Elevation of source: About 6,387 m
  • Length: 1,376 km
  • Basin area in India: About 3.66 lakh sq. km
  • Largest Himalayan tributary: Tons
  • Major Peninsular tributary: Chambal
  • Major right-bank tributaries: Tons, Giri, Chambal, Sindh, Betwa, Ken
  • Important left-bank tributaries: Hindon, Hanuman Ganga, Rind, Sengar
  • Important cities: Delhi, Mathura, Agra, Etawah
  • Confluence with Ganga: Prayagraj
  • Confluence name: Triveni Sangam
  • West-to-east order: Chambal → Sindh → Betwa → Ken
  • Important upper-course tributaries: Tons and Giri
  • Important project: Lakhwar-Vyasi
  • Important barrage: Hathnikund
  • Final drainage: Ganga → Bay of Bengal
