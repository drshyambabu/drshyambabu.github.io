Ganga River

Ganga River – one of the major river systems of India.

The Ganga is one of the most important river systems of India. It has great geographical, economic, agricultural, cultural and ecological significance.

Exam Focus: Origin, course, tributaries, river basin, major cities, important projects, delta and geographical features of the Ganga are important topics for UPSC, UPPSC and other competitive examinations.

1. Origin and Source

The Bhagirathi is a major headstream of the Ganga. It originates in the Gangotri Glacier region of Uttarakhand. The Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet at Devprayag in Uttarakhand. From Devprayag onward, the combined river is known as the Ganga.

Important for Exams: Devprayag is the confluence of the Bhagirathi and Alaknanda. After this confluence, the river is called the Ganga.

2. Course of the Ganga

After Devprayag, the Ganga descends from the Himalayan region and enters the plains at Haridwar in Uttarakhand.

It then flows through the plains of northern India, mainly through Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. The Ganga basin also includes a small part of Jharkhand before the river enters West Bengal.

In its lower course, the Ganga system divides into distributary channels. The Hooghly is an important distributary through West Bengal. The Ganga-Brahmaputra river system ultimately reaches the Bay of Bengal.

4. Ganga River Basin

The Ganga basin is one of the major river basins of the Indian subcontinent. It contains extensive fertile alluvial plains and supports a very large population.

The basin is extremely important for agriculture, irrigation, settlement, industries, transport and water resources.

The fertile alluvial deposits of the Ganga plain have made the region one of India's major agricultural areas.

6. Important Projects and Conservation

Various programmes and projects have been undertaken for pollution control, sewage treatment, river conservation and ecological improvement of the Ganga.

The National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG) is an important institution associated with the conservation and rejuvenation of the Ganga.

Important: Namami Gange is a major programme aimed at the conservation and rejuvenation of the Ganga and its ecosystem.

7. Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta

The Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems together form one of the world's largest deltaic regions.

The delta extends across parts of India and Bangladesh and includes the Sundarbans region, which is famous for its mangrove ecosystem.

Geography Fact: The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta is an important example of a large riverine delta formed by the deposition of sediments near the Bay of Bengal.
