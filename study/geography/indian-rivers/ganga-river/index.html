1. Origin and Formation

  • Source stream: Bhagirathi.
  • Source: Gangotri Glacier region, Uttarakhand.
  • Gaumukh: Snout of Gangotri Glacier and associated with the origin of the Bhagirathi.
  • Devprayag: Bhagirathi + Alaknanda = Ganga.
  • Length: About 2,525 km.
  • Outfall: Bay of Bengal.
Most Important: The river is called Bhagirathi at its source. After the confluence of Bhagirathi and Alaknanda at Devprayag, it is known as the Ganga.

2. Panch Prayag

Panch Prayag refers to the five major confluences of the Alaknanda river system in Uttarakhand.

Prayag Confluence Exam Point
Vishnuprayag Alaknanda + Dhauliganga First major Prayag in the downstream sequence.
Nandprayag Alaknanda + Nandakini Second Prayag.
Karnaprayag Alaknanda + Pindar Third Prayag.
Rudraprayag Alaknanda + Mandakini Fourth Prayag.
Devprayag Alaknanda + Bhagirathi Ganga is formed.
Memory Trick:
Vishnu → Nand → Karna → Rudra → Dev
Dhauliganga → Nandakini → Pindar → Mandakini → Bhagirathi

3. Course of the Ganga

  • After Devprayag, the river is called the Ganga.
  • It flows through Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and West Bengal.
  • The Ganga enters the northern plains at Haridwar.
  • In Uttar Pradesh, the Ganga enters from the Bijnor region.
  • Yamuna joins the Ganga at Prayagraj.
  • It continues eastward through Bihar and West Bengal.
  • At Farakka, the lower Ganga system divides into the Bhagirathi-Hooghly and Padma branches.
  • The Ganga-Brahmaputra-Meghna system ultimately drains into the Bay of Bengal.

4. Major Tributaries of the Ganga

Left-bank Tributaries

Tributary Important Exam Fact
Ramganga Important tributary of the Ganga; joins near Kannauj region.
Gomti Origin: Gomat Tal, Uttar Pradesh; joins Ganga in the Ghazipur region.
Ghaghra Major Himalayan tributary; known as Karnali in Nepal.
Gandak Major Himalayan tributary; joins the Ganga near Patna region.
Kosi Saptakoshi system; famous for frequent course shifting and heavy sediment load.
Mahananda Important left-bank tributary of the lower Ganga system.

Right-bank Tributaries

Tributary Important Exam Fact
Yamuna Largest and westernmost major tributary of the Ganga; joins at Prayagraj.
Son (Sone) Origin: Amarkantak region, Madhya Pradesh; major right-bank tributary.
Principal Tributaries — Must Remember:

Right Bank: Yamuna, Son

Left Bank: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghra, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda
UPPSC Exam Trap:
  • Tons is NOT a direct tributary of the Ganga. It is a tributary of the Yamuna.
  • Chambal, Sindh, Betwa and Ken belong to the Yamuna tributary system, not the direct Ganga tributary list.
  • Yamuna joins Ganga at Prayagraj, not Varanasi.
  • Bhagirathi + Alaknanda = Ganga occurs at Devprayag.

5. Ganga River Basin

Fact Data
Ganga Basin – total international area About 10.86 lakh sq. km
Indian part of Ganga Basin 8,38,803 sq. km
Share of India's geographical area About 26%
River length 2,525 km
Major Indian states along main course Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal
Geography: The Ganga Plain is an extensive alluvial plain formed mainly by deposition from the Ganga and its tributary systems. It is one of India's most important agricultural and densely populated regions.

7. Farakka and Lower Ganga

  • Farakka Barrage is located in West Bengal.
  • It regulates Ganga flow and diverts water through a feeder canal towards the Hooghly system.
  • Downstream of Farakka, the Ganga divides into Bhagirathi-Hooghly and Padma.
  • Padma flows into Bangladesh and later joins the Brahmaputra-Jamuna and Meghna system.
  • The combined river system finally drains into the Bay of Bengal.

9. Quick Revision Facts

  • Ganga source stream: Bhagirathi
  • Glacier: Gangotri Glacier
  • Gaumukh: Snout of Gangotri Glacier
  • Ganga formed at: Devprayag
  • Formation: Bhagirathi + Alaknanda
  • Enters plains: Haridwar
  • Enters Uttar Pradesh: Bijnor region
  • Yamuna joins Ganga: Prayagraj
  • Length: 2,525 km
  • Indian Ganga Basin: 8,38,803 sq. km
  • Right-bank principal tributaries: Yamuna, Son
  • Left-bank principal tributaries: Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghra, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda
  • Kosi: Important for course shifting and heavy sediment load
  • Son: Major right-bank tributary; originates in the Amarkantak region
  • Farakka: Important barrage in West Bengal
  • Hooghly: Important distributary branch of the lower Ganga system
  • Final outlet: Bay of Bengal

10. One-Line Master Revision

Gangotri → Bhagirathi → Devprayag → Ganga → Haridwar → Bijnor → Prayagraj (Yamuna) → Bihar → Farakka → Bhagirathi-Hooghly / Padma → Bay of Bengal
